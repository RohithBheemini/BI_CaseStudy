Step 1 • Exploratory Data Analysis (.ipynb)

Why We Couldn't Just Load the Raw CSV into Power BI

When we inspected the raw dataset in Python, we found 4 major data problems that would ruin any dashboard if left uncleaned:
1. 4,225 Orders Had Missing Values (9.3%)
Missing values were typed as text "NaN". If we simply deleted incomplete rows, we would throw away nearly 10% of Zomato's business!
2. Fake Ratings (6.0★) & Underage Riders (Age 15)
We caught a broken batch of 520 rows where vehicle condition was "3", ratings were 6.0 out of 5, and rider age was 15.
3. GPS Coordinates in the Ocean (0, 0)
3,640 orders had restaurant GPS at (0, 0) and 431 had negative signs—making delivery distances look like 6,884 km!
4. Excel Decimal Times (0.458333)
Over 4,000 timestamps were saved as decimals like 0.75 instead of 18:00 (6:00 PM), breaking kitchen wait-time math.
What to say: "If you put messy data into Power BI, you get wrong answers. In my Jupyter Notebook, I found 4,225 incomplete rows, impossible 6-star ratings, 3,640 GPS points at zero-zero in the ocean, and 4,000 decimal timestamps."
Step 2 • Smart Data Cleaning (zomato_delivery_cleaned.csv)

How We Fixed Every Bug Without Losing Any Orders

  • 1. Used Rider IDs to Recover Cities & GPS Locations: Every Rider ID starts with the city & restaurant code (like RANCHIRES02DEL01 = Ranchi, Restaurant #2). We used this to fill missing cities and restore all 3,640 zero-GPS locations!
  • 2. True Delivery Distance is Now 1.5 km to 21 km: Once GPS signs and zero-coordinates were fixed, every single order showed a realistic city distance (averaging 9.7 km instead of 6,884 km!).
  • 3. Converted Decimal Times to Real Clock Times: We converted decimals like 0.75 into 18:00 and found that kitchen pickup wait time is always 5, 10, or 15 minutes (averaging 10 minutes).
  • 4. Fixed Fake Ratings & Ages: Replaced impossible 6.0★ ratings and Age 15 with the realistic middle value (4.7★ rating and Age 30), keeping all 45,584 orders 100% clean.
What to say: "Instead of deleting rows, we used smart clues already inside the data—like the city code inside the Rider ID—to fix the GPS coordinates, convert decimal times, and export a 100% clean dataset (`zomato_delivery_cleaned.csv`) right into our folder."
